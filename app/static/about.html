About AmazingSpider

AmazingSpider is a computerised version of Spider, the traditional two-pack card patience—faster and more fun on a phone. The Android release on Google Play is proAmazingSpider, published by SuperAce.

What the game is

Spider uses 54 cards in the tableau at the start: four columns of six cards and six columns of five. The remaining 50 cards form a stock; when no move is possible, you deal ten at a time. The goal is to build each suit from king down to ace, then remove a completed suit of 13 cards by tapping the king.

The app adds a clear HUD (Moves, Stack, Done), color-coded suits, and online sessions if you choose—see our Privacy Policy for data handling.

Rules & AmazingSpider differences

In classic Spider you move one card unless a run is the same suit in descending order. You may move onto a card of the next higher rank, or into an empty column. Kings may only enter empty columns.

AmazingSpider also allows moving a mixed-suit column when there is a logical path. The Solve button joins same-suit fragments—especially useful near the end of a deal.

Visually: blue-and-white backs, green clubs, deep blue spades, orange diamonds, a felt-style table with a light Amazing Spider watermark, and a colored toolbar for New, Deal, Solve, Undo, and Help.

Strategy: empty columns

Empty columns are valuable. Avoid moving kings into them early—once there, a king is hard to move again. Prefer stacking as much as you can onto columns already headed by a king to free other columns.

Suits, kings & Google Play

Where a column is not headed by a king, organise by suit when you can to help later moves. Under kings, there is less hurry to sort—prioritise creating empty columns, and only move kings when nothing better is available.
